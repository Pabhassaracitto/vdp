## Critical Terminology Questions

### 1. Sobhana (浄好) - Module M3
**Pāli:** Sobhana  
**Japanese:** 浄好 (jōkō)  
**Context:** Used in "Sobhana cetasikas" (25 beautiful mental factors)

**Question:** Is "浄好" (pure/beautiful) the best translation for "Sobhana"?
- Alternative 1: 美善心所 (bishin-shinjo) - "beautiful wholesome mental factors"
- Alternative 2: 清浄心所 (shōjō-shinjo) - "pure mental factors"
- Current choice: 浄好 (jōkō) - "pure beauty"

**Recommendation needed:** Which term best captures both the aesthetic and ethical dimensions of Sobhana in Japanese Buddhist context?

### 2. Viratiyo (戒部分) - Module M3
**Pāli:** Viratiyo  
**Japanese:** 戒部分 (kai-bubun) - literally "precept part"  
**Context:** Three abstinences (right speech, right action, right livelihood)

**Question:** Is "戒部分" (precept part) appropriate for "Viratiyo"?
- Alternative 1: 離心所 (ri-shinjo) - "abstinence mental factors"
- Alternative 2: 三離 (sanri) - "three abstentions"
- Current choice: 戒部分 (kai-bubun) - "precept section"

**Concern:** "戒部分" may be too literal and miss the active nature of "virati" (abstaining/refraining).

---

### 3. Vipāka (果報) - Modules M5, M12
**Pāli:** Vipāka  
**Japanese:** 果報 (kahō)  
**Context:** Resultant consciousness, result of kamma

**Question:** Is "果報" (kahō - fortune/fate) the correct term for "Vipāka"?
- Alternative 1: 異熟 (ijuku) - "ripened result" (more technical)
- Alternative 2: 果報心 (kahō-shin) - "resultant consciousness"
- Current choice: 果報 (kahō) - common in Japanese Buddhism

**Concern:** "果報" in Japanese often means "fortune" or "fate" rather than the technical Abhidhamma meaning of "result of kamma."

---

### 4. Kiriyā/Kiriya (唯作) - Modules M5, M14
**Pāli:** Kiriyā / Kiriya  
**Japanese:** 唯作 (yuisa)  
**Context:** Functional consciousness that produces no kammic result

**Question:** Is "唯作" (yuisa - "only making") the standard Japanese term for "Kiriya"?
- Alternative 1: 機能 (kinō) - "function" (more modern)
- Alternative 2: 無記 (muki) - "indeterminate" (but this is different concept)
- Current choice: 唯作 (yuisa) - traditional term

**Note:** This appears to be the standard term in Japanese Abhidhamma texts.

---

### 5. Ahetuka (無因) - Modules M9, M12, M16
**Pāli:** Ahetuka  
**Japanese:** 無因 (muin)  
**Context:** Rootless consciousness (18 types)

**Question:** Is "無因" (muin - "without cause") accurate for "Ahetuka"?
- Alternative 1: 無根 (mukon) - "without root" (more literal)
- Alternative 2: 因なし (in-nashi) - "no causes" (plain Japanese)
- Current choice: 無因 (muin) - "no causes"

**Concern:** "Ahetuka" literally means "without hetu (root/causes)" - should we use "無根" (without root) instead of "無因" (without cause)?

### 6. Appamaññā (無量) - Module M3

- Current choice: 無量 (muryō) - "immeasurable"

**Note:** "無量" is the standard term in Japanese Buddhism for the brahmavihāras.
